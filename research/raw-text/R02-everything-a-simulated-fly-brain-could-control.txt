The Unlimited Body: Everything a Simulated Fly
Brain Could Possibly Control
Research cutoff: October 27, 2026


The central answer is gloriously weird but technically straightforward:


A connectome-derived nervous system does not mathematically require the body evolution gave it.
Once neural state is represented digitally, it can be coupled to any computable dynamical system: six legs,
eight tentacles, a humanoid, a drone, a colony, a reaction–diffusion field, a cloud of particles, a topology-
changing mesh, a musical instrument, a game, a living painting, a mathematical ecosystem, or a creature
that eats magenta pixels and shits crystalline glitter.


But there is a gigantic scientific trap hiding inside that freedom. The easier it becomes to make the
transplanted nervous system control an arbitrary world, the harder it becomes to prove the nervous
system is actually responsible for the interesting behavior. The most important paper for this entire
question may therefore be one that intentionally built a biological abomination: Brunton and colleagues
connected the 302-neuron C. elegans connectome to a simulated fruit-fly body and trained an artificial
decoder until the chimera walked like a fly. It worked. And that was the point: realistic behavior can emerge
from a biologically nonsensical brain–body pairing if the interface is powerful enough. 1


That result radically reframes the problem. The frontier is no longer merely, “Can a fly connectome control
X?” In an engineering sense, the answer is almost always yes.


The deeper question is:


       How alien can X become while the biological wiring still makes a measurable,
       nontrivial, causally necessary contribution to what the resulting organism does?


What a simulated fly brain actually is
The phrase “simulated fly brain” currently refers to several profoundly different objects. Confusing them
produces most of the hype and a lot of the bullshit.


FlyWire / FAFB is fundamentally an anatomical dataset. The adult female FlyWire connectome published in
2024 contains 139,255 proofread neurons and roughly 54.5 million chemical-synapse contacts. It
includes neuronal morphology, synaptic locations, extensive cell-type annotations and synapse-level
neurotransmitter predictions. The publication explicitly states that the main connectome contains chemical
rather than electrical synapses; reliable identification of gap junctions awaits appropriate data. A single
directed connection between two neurons can contain many synaptic contacts, so Codex “connection”
counts must not be confused with raw synapse counts. 2

BANC — Brain And Nerve Cord — is a different beast. Bates and colleagues’ 2026 Nature paper
reconstructs a unified adult female brain, neck connective and ventral nerve cord. The biological
architecture it reveals is extraordinarily relevant here: roughly 140,000 brain neurons and 20,000 VNC
neurons communicate through about 1,300 descending and 1,800 ascending neurons, while local sensor–
effector loops perform a large fraction of body-specific control. Higher learning/navigation regions appear
more supervisory than equivalent to a centralized executive issuing every muscle command. That is
precisely the sort of hierarchical organization that makes body transplantation plausible. 3


BANC is still not a scan of every piece of the organism. The published volume lacks the lamina and ocellar
ganglion; the authors estimate that around 9,390 R1–R6 photoreceptors and Lai cells associated with the
lamina are consequently absent. Sensory neurons entering the CNS are represented extensively, but
complete peripheral sensory endings, muscle physiology and every neuromuscular interaction are not
magically supplied by having a complete central connectome. 3


MaleCNS is the corresponding landmark male dataset. Janelia’s project released v1.0 on June 8, 2026, with
the associated Cell publication appearing on September 3, 2026. Official descriptions put it at
approximately 166,700 neurons and 125 million synaptic contacts, spanning the adult male brain and
ventral nerve cord through an intact neck connective. Its great advantage for synthetic embodiment is that
sensory-to-brain-to-VNC-to-motor routes can be investigated in one specimen rather than splicing separate
brain and cord connectomes together. 4


As of October 2026, FlyWire Codex is an analysis/access layer for several of these datasets, not a neural
simulator. Its current dataset pages distinguish FAFB/FlyWire, BANC and MaleCNS and expose annotations,
connectivity, morphology and transmitter information; downloadable/static data are preferable for large
programmatic analyses. neuPrint likewise provides graph-oriented storage, querying and visualization for
connectomic datasets. Neither causes neurons to spike merely because you loaded a graph. 5

Shiu et al. is the key bridge between anatomy and dynamics. Their 2024 work placed essentially the
adult FlyWire brain into a leaky integrate-and-fire model informed by connectivity and transmitter
identity. Researchers can activate defined neurons at controlled rates, silence populations, and record
propagated spikes throughout the network. They showed that stimulation of taste-related neurons
predicted downstream feeding-related activity and used the model to generate experimentally testable
feeding and grooming predictions. The public repository exposes the actual simulation machinery. 6


But Shiu’s model is deliberately simplified. The paper explicitly notes that it ignores morphology-dependent
neural dynamics and receptor dynamics, among other limitations. The code converts anatomical
information into simplified point-neuron dynamics; it does not reconstruct every channel, dendrite, synaptic
receptor or physiological state of the original animal. 7


flyvis / Lappalainen et al. takes almost the opposite strategy. Rather than simply running generic
dynamics across the whole brain, it constructs a connectome-constrained model of the visual system and
trains parameters against visual computation. That can produce greater functional fidelity in one subsystem
while covering much less of the nervous system. A whole-brain model is therefore not automatically “more
biological” than a carefully constrained subcircuit model. 8


NeuroMechFly v2 / FlyGym supplies the body side. It is a detailed physics-based adult fly platform with
articulated anatomy, sensory interfaces and MuJoCo-based embodiment. NeuroMechFly is not itself FlyWire
running inside a fly; it is a body/environment into which many kinds of controllers can be inserted. 9


Eon Systems’ March 2026 experiment combined those worlds. Eon integrated a Shiu-derived whole-brain
LIF model, a connectome-constrained visual system and the NeuroMechFly body. Their technical description
says the neural and body systems exchange information at roughly 15-ms intervals. Taste-like signals and
fictive dust stimulate identified sensory pathways; selected descending/motor-related outputs are
interpreted as behavioral commands; lower-level NeuroMechFly controllers generate detailed body
movement; and movement alters subsequent sensory input. The demonstration includes approach toward
food, grooming and feeding. 10


The crucial caveat is that Eon did not wire every biological motor neuron directly to an individually modeled
muscle and simply watch a fly awaken. Its bridge is deliberately low dimensional. Selected pathways such as
steering/forward-related descending neurons feed existing lower-level controllers; much of the complex
joint-level movement is therefore contributed by those engineered controllers and the simulated body. Eon

also explicitly identifies missing internal state, plasticity, learning, hormonal modulation, diverse cell
biophysics and other biological dynamics. 11


That is still a real milestone. It is a closed connectome-informed sensorimotor loop. It just is not the same
thing as demonstrating that connectome topology plus generic neuronal equations is sufficient to
reconstruct an autonomous animal.


Two 2026 projects push the concept even farther from natural fly behavior.


FlyGM instantiates the fly connectome as a graph-structured artificial controller and trains it with deep
reinforcement learning to operate a biomechanical fly. It reports walking, turning, gait initiation and flight,
and importantly compares the biological graph with degree-preserving rewires, random graphs and
conventional MLPs. The biological topology exhibits better sample efficiency and performance in the
reported experiments. That is evidence for useful structural inductive bias—not evidence that the trained
edge computations are the original fly’s physiology. 12


And on October 7, 2026, Cho, Kang and Kim posted an even stranger result: they use MaleCNS v1.0 as a
fixed recurrent topology, learn a scalar on each anatomical edge, and train separate networks on
bounded arithmetic and a controlled grounded relational-language task. The MaleCNS topology reached
92.77% held-out bounded-addition accuracy versus 67.93% for degree-preserving rewired controls, and
61.59% on their strict language endpoint versus 44.17% for matched rewires. Their limitations section is
admirably explicit: the model omits transmitter signs, delays, realistic neuronal dynamics and biological
plasticity. In other words, this is emphatically not a fly discovering arithmetic. It is evidence that a
biological connectome can be an unexpectedly reusable computational architecture. 13


What the anatomy still does not tell us. Even the spectacular 2026 datasets do not specify all per-cell
membrane conductances, voltage-gated channel densities, cable properties, release probabilities, short-
term synaptic dynamics, complete postsynaptic receptor identities, electrical connections, conduction
delays, neuropeptide and volume-transmission dynamics, glial regulation, hormonal state, metabolic state,
developmental history, learned synaptic state, sensory-receptor transduction functions, muscle force curves
or every peripheral connection. Neurotransmitter identity alone does not determine the exact postsynaptic
response, because receptor identity and cellular context matter. FlyWire’s own publication explicitly excludes
electrical synapses from the main reconstructed graph. 14


So: can a connectome simulation spontaneously generate behavior? It can generate recurrent activity
under suitable dynamics, but the published Shiu framework demonstrates behaviorally relevant processing
primarily by stimulating or perturbing selected populations. Eon’s embodiment is closed-loop but cue
driven. FlyGM becomes competent through reinforcement learning. No current result demonstrates that a
static adult connectome plus generic LIF parameters, started up like a laptop, spontaneously reconstructs
the whole natural behavioral organization of a living fly. 15

DOOMFLY, by nftechie, feeds Doom frames into engineered sensory populations of a MaleCNS v1.0
simulation and maps neural activity to movement, turning and firing. An experimental dopamine-gated
memory rule changes a limited subset of connections. This is genuinely dynamic and closed-loop, but
thousands of game rounds had not produced convincing evidence of competent autonomous Doom
learning in contemporary reporting. 18


FlyDoom goes farther scientifically by comparing MaleCNS-constrained recurrent controllers with rewired
graphs and ordinary neural networks on simplified VizDoom tasks. That is the correct direction because it
asks whether the connectome itself matters rather than merely demonstrating that something connected
to Doom moves. 16


Fly64, by ornata, connects a MaleCNS-derived system to Super Mario 64. It is a genuine controller
experiment with a dashboard and local setup, but the game/sensory semantics still have to be engineered.
 19




The viral Beat Saber experiment is particularly instructive. The original September 9 post by @lyraaaa is
real, but the author clarified that the system was overfit to one track and used replay/external song
information; it was not simply watching incoming blocks through fly eyes and spontaneously mastering
rhythm combat. The author explicitly described further work as reducing reliance on those external signals.
So this is a fascinating trained interface—not evidence of a natural fly brain understanding Beat Saber. 20


Fly Brain Minecraft runs filtered MaleCNS graphs for fly mobs and supplies explicit synthetic signals for
environmental events such as food, threats and weather, with motor readouts and neural HUDs. It is closer
to an artificial ecology than a neuroscience experiment. 16


fly-flappy reportedly runs the retained MaleCNS graph at 50 Hz, feeds it engineered visual input and either
reads DNp01 directly or trains a logistic action decoder to flap. This is an excellent example of why “full
connectome” is not synonymous with “fully biological”—the graph may be biological while the interface
remains an engineered prosthesis. 16


FlyPong, by Jonatas Peraza, deserves special attention because it reports a failure. It uses a MaleCNS
subgraph, feeds ball information into the neural system and tests dopamine-inspired plasticity. The
attempted learning rule did not produce convincing learning. Negative results like this are
disproportionately valuable because they puncture the assumption that sprinkling “dopamine” onto a
connectome automatically produces an adaptive animal. 21


Fly Dino uses only an approximately 80-neuron MaleCNS circuit, simplified leaky-tanh dynamics and a
trainable 243-parameter action readout. It reports held-out tests and circuit-silencing experiments. That
small, explicit interface is in some ways scientifically cleaner than running 166,000 neurons and then
allowing a gigantic decoder to do whatever the hell it wants. 16


Fly Self Driving reportedly retains about 165,000 MaleCNS neurons and learns connection gains/leak
parameters to steer from a low-resolution windscreen. Its authors report comparisons with a rewired
connectome, linear controller and MLP, which makes it much more informative than an isolated steering
video. At the same time, learning a huge number of gains means the resulting computation should be
described as connectome-constrained machine learning, not an untouched male fly driving a car. 16

fly-cartpole uses a 5,459-neuron flight-stabilization-related subset, tunes only a handful of sensory gains,
and reports large improvement over random perturbation controls. This is exactly the sort of
transplantation experiment worth pursuing: take a circuit whose evolutionary job is stabilization and ask

Doodle Fly runs a Shiu-style whole-FlyWire LIF simulation, artificially stimulates LC10a according to target-
platform location and reads several descending-neuron classes to press left/right controls. Nothing is
trained, and its author reports eye-swap and blind controls. That makes it a particularly nice demonstration
of fixed biological network + artificial sensor + artificial motor prosthesis. 16


20fly8 uses a mushroom-body-derived FlyWire circuit rather than the whole brain. Board state is encoded
through projection-neuron channels, measured PN→Kenyon-cell and Kenyon-cell→MBON connectivity
forms the substrate, and a project-defined dopamine/TD rule modifies the effective KC→MBON pathway.
The author reports random, rewired and shuffled controls. Conceptually, this is much closer to the
aesthetic-preference experiments you are imagining than Doom is. 16


Banana Quest uses the full FlyWire graph, converts an odor-like gradient into antennal receptor activity
and derives turn/feed/startle behavior from descending/SEZ-related populations. Its documentation
explicitly distinguishes measured wiring from artificial sensory and readout mappings—good scientific
hygiene. 16


FLYT3 runs a retained MaleCNS LIF graph with a trained descending/VNC readout. Its reported wildfire-
classification experiment is an unusually useful embarrassment: the connectome system scores about
60.8%, while a pooled-pixel MLP reaches about 85.7% on the same five-class task. Biological wiring is
therefore not some magical universal computer that automatically beats ordinary ML. 16


fly-swing uses the MaleCNS giant-fiber escape circuit—LC4/LPLC2→DNp01—as the biologically grounded
event that initiates evasive behavior, while an external k-nearest-neighbor threat memory helps determine
web-swinging behavior. It is a wonderful example of mixed authorship: the connectome contributes a real
escape-like signal, but “Spider-Man fly” is largely an engineered downstream organism. 16


Fly-NAF places an entire FlyWire-based LIF simulation inside Five Nights at Freddy’s, but augments it with
hand-built screen mappings, memory and starvation timers. That is a real hybrid artificial organism—not a
clean test that the fly itself learned FNAF. 16


Katamari Connectome maps a large FlyWire-derived neural subset into Katamari Damacy controls with no
RL training, but its reported evidence is a single session with no matched baseline. Fun as hell; scientifically
thin. 16

The body is an interface, not a destiny
The strongest biological argument that a fly-derived nervous system could inhabit something radically non-
fly-shaped comes from the fly itself.


BANC shows that motor organization is distributed: local sensory information strongly influences nearby
effectors, while ascending and descending neurons coordinate those modules across the animal. One
descending neuron can influence multiple body parts or physiological systems rather than encoding one
literal muscle contraction. 3


That means there is no requirement that an artificial transplant preserve a one-neuron→one-muscle
correspondence.


A useful abstraction is:


           world → E(sense) → Nconnectome → zintent → Dmotor → Clocal → Pbody → world

where:


E is a synthetic sensory organ,
N is the connectome-derived nervous system,
z is a deliberately low-dimensional neural bottleneck,
D translates that bottleneck into body-level goals,
C contains local reflex/CPG/limb controllers, and
P is the body/world physics.


The architecture can range from biologically conservative to hilariously alien.


A direct mapping might connect left-turn-related descending neurons to a robot’s left/right steering
differential.


A motor-synergy mapping could interpret dozens of neural outputs as a handful of latent variables:
forward drive, turn bias, approach, retreat, flight/escape intensity, feeding, grasping, phase, stiffness.


A hierarchical decoder can expand those variables into hundreds or thousands of joint, muscle or material
commands.


A reservoir-style interface can read a large fraction of whole-connectome activity and train an arbitrary
downstream policy.


A co-adaptive system can allow the decoder, body and eventually selected neural weights to adapt
simultaneously.


Capability generally increases as you move downward. Biological interpretability generally decreases.

The importance of this distinction is beautifully demonstrated by the Digital Sphinx. Brunton and
colleagues took a C. elegans network of only 302 neurons, supplied fly-body proprioception to its sensory
neurons through a random projection, and trained a variational encoder/decoder with a 16-dimensional
latent space to convert worm motor activity into commands for 42 fly leg actuators. The resulting animal
produced realistic coordinated fly walking. The authors explicitly argue that this does not illuminate worm
or fly motor biology; an expressive downstream policy can make a biologically meaningless intermediate
network look astonishingly competent. 1


So yes: a fly brain can technically control a spider, snake, fish, bird, quadruped, humanoid, drone, soft
robot, amorphous blob, particle swarm or hundreds of limbs.


What changes is the interface.


Snake: fly output controls wave phase, curvature amplitude and propagation direction of a body CPG.


Fish: neural outputs specify tail-beat frequency/asymmetry, pectoral-fin bias and depth goal.


Bird: outputs control wingbeat amplitude/frequency, left–right asymmetry and tail attitude.


Drone: outputs become thrust, yaw, pitch and altitude goals while a conventional flight controller solves
millisecond stabilization.


Quadruped: fly outputs set gait, velocity and turning commands while local leg policies solve foot
placement.


Humanoid: the September 2026 preprint already demonstrates this category using a 3,609-state fly-
inspired recurrent controller connected to a simulated Unitree G1. It completes 61 of 63 evaluated
conditions, although intervention analysis shows that its effective locomotor pathway relies heavily on
body/command input and persistent motor state rather than demonstrating a rich biological sensory
hierarchy. 17


Soft blob: outputs control pressure gradients, stiffness fields, contraction waves or material-growth fronts
rather than joints.


Hundreds of appendages: outputs need not address appendages individually. A few scalars can
parameterize a distributed field or identical local limb controllers.


Several bodies simultaneously: the nervous system can broadcast a common behavioral state to multiple
bodies while receiving aggregated sensory feedback. The resulting entity is mathematically no stranger
than a colonial organism with distributed effectors.


The octopus architecture

The most credible fly-brain octopus should not ask a fly connectome to directly discover thousands of
artificial muscle commands. That would mostly become a reinforcement-learning stunt.

Instead:


Neural substrate. Use MaleCNS or BANC because both contain brain↔cord circuitry rather than only a
brain. Initially freeze all biological effective weights.


Synthetic perception. Preserve natural correspondence wherever possible. Motion and looming can enter
visual pathways; contact/strain can enter proprioceptive or mechanosensory populations; odor-like
resource fields can enter olfactory pathways; contact nutrient signals can enter gustatory pathways. Any
truly octopus-specific variable should be labeled as an artificial sensory mapping rather than given fake
biological semantics.


Intent layer. Read perhaps 16–64 dimensions from descending/VNC/motor-associated neural populations.
Candidate dimensions could include locomotor drive, lateral bias, approach/avoidance, escape intensity,
feeding/contact state, exploration, action phase and several learned latent variables. The exact
dimensionality is an experimental knob, not a biological fact.


Eight local arms. Each arm gets its own lower controller for bending, torsion, extension, shortening,
stiffness and contact reflexes. Octopus arms are an especially good model because real octopus control is
itself unusually distributed, and recent octopus-inspired robotics uses peripheral sensing/control to handle
high-dimensional flexible limbs. 26


Shared fly influence. The neural bottleneck modulates arm goals. For instance:



                                            ai = Ci (D(zt , si , gt ))

where zt is fly neural state, si is local arm sensory state and gt is global body state.


The eight arms can share one permutation-equivariant local policy. Consequently, the body can lose an arm,
grow a ninth, or temporarily split one arm into five branches without rewriting the fly brain.


Local reflexes. Contact should produce immediate arm-level withdrawal or stiffening before waiting for the
entire brain loop. A compressed report of that event then returns through synthetic ascending channels.


That is not cheating. Biological animals already distribute control across peripheral/spinal/VNC circuitry.
BANC specifically supports a view in which local sensory–effector modules are coordinated rather than
micromanaged by the brain. 3


Training. Freeze the fly network first. Train only the motor adapter and arm controllers using imitation
learning, reinforcement learning, model-predictive control or differentiable simulation.


Then freeze those too and ask the scientifically meaningful questions:


Does silencing particular fly populations predictably alter which arm reaches?


Does replacing the intact connectome with a degree-preserving rewire degrade behavior?

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 12 / 30>
Does replaying the same neural trajectory reproduce the same high-level strategy?


Does replacing zt with noise leave performance unchanged?


How much mutual information remains between fly state and future arm trajectories after conditioning on
current body state?


Does the same decoder generalize to a ninth arm?


Those tests tell you whether the fly is directing the octopus or merely riding inside a robot that already
knows what to do.


Could the neural network itself eventually learn its new body? Not under a static Shiu-like model. A
frozen network can generate different activity because its sensory loop changed, but its synapses do not
magically recalibrate. A trainable artificial decoder can learn the body while the fly remains fixed. To make
learning happen inside the biological substrate, you must add synaptic plasticity or other adaptive neuronal
state. The mushroom body is the obvious first place for biologically motivated reward learning; wholesale
remapping of arbitrary octopus motor degrees of freedom would require additional plasticity assumptions
that current whole-CNS models do not yet justify. 27


Could it develop a recognizable individual style?


Absolutely, operationally. Even a fixed recurrent network interacting with a nonlinear body can develop
repeatable biases in timing, exploration and arm use. Adding history-dependent synaptic changes, internal
drives or morphology makes persistent idiosyncrasy even more likely.


But “individual behavioral style” is defensible.


“This is the personality of the scanned fly” is not.


The current connectomes are snapshots of single animals, but the models omit enormous amounts of
individual physiological state.


When the body is not a body
Here is where the design space goes absolutely feral.


There is no mathematical rule saying neural output must become torque.


An actuator is simply a variable that changes the state-transition equation of a world.


So a neural spike can cause:

or just as legitimately:

The distinction between a robot and a living painting is therefore not mathematical. It is semantic and
causal.


A mathematical body could consist entirely of parameters controlling strange attractors, iterated-function
systems, cellular automata, signed-distance fields, level sets, L-systems, wave equations or graph
grammars. The nervous system might alter coefficients that determine whether the entity folds, branches,
curls, bifurcates or becomes chaotic.


A particle body can contain millions of elements even if the brain exports only twenty signals. Those twenty
values can control force-field centers, attraction/repulsion strengths, cohesion, vortex intensity, split/merge
thresholds and material state. A million particles then collectively enact the neural decision.


A chemical body can use reaction–diffusion equations. Neural variables can modulate feed rates, catalyst
concentration, diffusion constants or reaction thresholds. Sensor channels report gradients and reaction
fronts back to the network.


A light body can consist of refractive-index fields, procedural interference, simulated diffraction, emissive
surfaces, anisotropic sparkle and caustics. Neural outputs need not “move” anything; action becomes
optical transformation.


A topological body can change connectivity. It can punch tunnels through itself, merge components,
detach pieces, braid filaments, form knots or change genus. A neural command might mean “permit a
topology-changing surgery in the maximally activated local region,” while lower physics decides the
geometry.

Experimental roadmap, scientific boundaries and deliverables
The most scientifically powerful version of this project would proceed in an almost perversely conservative
order while the body becomes increasingly insane.


Freeze the biological substrate first. Pick an exact MaleCNS, BANC or FlyWire release. Record neuron
filters, connection thresholds, transmitter/sign rules and simulator parameters. Do not quietly retrain
millions of internal weights and then announce that the original fly figured something out.


Introduce one artificial sense at a time. Begin with visual motion, looming, odor-like gradients and touch
because those have defensible mappings. Then introduce curvature. Then color/chroma. Then novelty. Each
new modality gets sensory-shuffle controls.


Expose an explicit neural bottleneck. The downstream body controller should receive a logged vector
from identifiable neural populations. A hidden 100-million-parameter network reading the entire
connectome is scientifically useless for attribution even if the resulting creature is magnificent.


Train the body interface while the brain is frozen. This isolates body calibration from neural learning.


Then freeze the body interface. Run matched intact, rewired, shuffled and random controllers.


Only then add plasticity. Start with mushroom-body-like reward learning rather than allowing arbitrary
backpropagation across the entire connectome.


Only after that evolve morphology. Let an evolutionary optimizer change tentacle number, stiffness,
sensor placement, growth cost, particle cohesion or material phase transitions while the nervous system
remains constrained.


This creates one of the weirdest and most interesting experiments available:


       Instead of asking whether a fly nervous system can adapt to an alien body, let alien
       bodies evolve around the fly nervous system.


FlyGM and the 2026 whole-connectome cognition results already suggest that natural connectome
topology can provide nontrivial inductive bias outside the exact task on which evolution originally shaped it,
although both results rely on substantial artificial learning machinery. 29


The minimum benchmark suite should contain:


intact connectome; degree-preserving rewire; shuffled edge weights/signs; matched random
recurrent network; decoder-only controller with the neural bottleneck replaced by noise or a
constant; and targeted biological-pathway ablations.


And every experiment should report trainable parameter counts separately for:

connectome core,
motor decoder,
local body controllers.

What neuroscience actually supports as of October 27, 2026: adult fly brains and whole central nervous
systems have been reconstructed at synaptic resolution; simplified whole-brain dynamics can make
experimentally useful sensorimotor predictions; connectome-constrained visual models can predict visual-
system responses; graph architectures copied from connectomes can help trained embodied policies;
connectome-derived systems can be closed around physics worlds; and biological topology can sometimes
confer measurable advantages over controlled rewires on tasks very unlike natural fly behavior. 30


What is experimentally plausible but not yet demonstrated as a mature biological model: a fly-derived
CNS controlling an eight-arm soft body through hierarchical adapters; morphologically changing
organisms; abstract field/particle/chemical bodies; persistent learned aesthetic appetites; multiple
connectomes sharing bodies or ecosystems; co-evolution of body morphology around a fixed biological
graph; and autonomous connectome-driven generative art in which aesthetic consequences feed back into
future learned action.


What is not established: that current connectome simulations preserve the scanned fly’s identity; that
generic LIF dynamics reconstruct its complete mental state; that arbitrary game success means the fly
network understands the game; that a connectome-driven artwork experiences aesthetic pleasure; or that
present projects demonstrate consciousness or literal whole-brain emulation. Current anatomical
connectomes omit too much physiological, molecular and learned state for those conclusions, and the
Digital Sphinx shows that behavioral appearance alone is an extremely weak test of biological fidelity. 31


The profound result is therefore not:
