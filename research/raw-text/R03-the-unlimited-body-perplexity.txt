The Unlimited Body
Executive scientific reality check
A connectome-derived nervous system can be connected to almost any programmable
environment—not just an insect body. What has not been demonstrated is that biological
wiring alone supplies the perception, motor competence, learning rules, or motivations
needed to inhabit every such environment. Existing systems already combine measured fly
connectivity with engineered sensory encoders, neural simulations, action decoders, and
virtual worlds; those interfaces contribute substantially to the resulting behavior. [1]
The promising design space is therefore not “a complete fly mind operating an arbitrary
puppet.” It is an experimentally inspectable hybrid organism: biological network structure,
modeled neural dynamics, invented anatomy, autonomous material physics, and explicitly
identified artificial learning. Eon’s technical account, community game controllers, and
browser experiments all illustrate different versions of this separation. [2] [3] [1]
This report distinguishes four evidence levels:

Do not compare counts without comparing definitions. Anatomical contacts, presynaptic sites,
postsynaptic partners, unique directed neuron-pair edges, retained neurons, and proofread
neurons are different quantities; community packages also apply filtering and aggregation. [10]
[1]

For example, DOOMFLY reports 166,700 retained neurons and 25,582,938 directed connections,
whereas the MaleCNS publication announcement reports about 125 million synaptic
connections. Those figures describe different counting units, not necessarily contradictory
reconstructions. [8] [2]

BANC’s important conceptual result
BANC is especially relevant to your octopus question because its analysis emphasizes
distributed control: local sensory–effector loops are linked through ascending and descending
pathways, while learning and navigation systems supervise those circuits. That is a stronger
architectural analogy for alien bodies than “one central brain directly commands every
muscle.” [12] [13]
It does not establish that a fly’s cord can operate an octopus arm without adaptation. It suggests
a useful engineering division: central selection and coordination, local sensorimotor
competence, and feedback between them.

What anatomy leaves unknown
An anatomical graph does not directly specify the living system’s membrane potentials,
channel states, receptor-dependent synaptic effects, hormonal condition, neuromodulatory
concentrations, or moment-to-moment plasticity. Synapse count is informative, but it is not the
same measurement as physiological synaptic strength. [1]
A useful missing-information ledger is:

                    Missing layer                                             Why it matters
     Ion channels and compartmental dynamics   Determine excitability, integration, adaptation, and dendritic computation

     Receptor identity and kinetics            A transmitter label alone does not fully specify the postsynaptic response

     Short-term synaptic dynamics              Repeated activity can facilitate or depress transmission

     Neuromodulation and peptides              Change gain, state, behavioral priorities, and plasticity

     Long-term plasticity rules                Necessary for particular forms of learned adaptation

     Developmental and individual history      A wiring snapshot is not a recovered lifetime

     Sensory transduction                      Pixels are not automatically photoreceptor currents

     Peripheral mechanics and physiology       Muscles, receptors, viscera, and body dynamics participate in control

     Ongoing internal state                    Hunger, arousal, reproductive state, and recent experience alter responses

These are central limitations acknowledged in Eon’s technical account, not minor
implementation details.

Does it act spontaneously?
In the original Shiu model, baseline firing is zero: input is delivered to selected neurons, and
downstream activity is then measured. Its successful sensorimotor predictions therefore do not
imply an autonomously wandering, unstimulated animal. Shiu et al., 2024
A different implementation can add background drive, stochastic activity, autonomous body
oscillators, or engineered exploration. Fly64 explicitly adds steady background input and
repeatable noise, so not every movement is caused by the screen. Fly64 implementation
The right question is not merely “does it move by itself?” It is:
        Which state variables generate the movement, and which of those variables are causally
        influenced by measured biological connectivity?

The Flyvis result is a particularly important counterpoint to both extremes: connectivity is not
sufficient to specify every unknown dynamical parameter, but anatomy plus a relevant task can
constrain useful mechanistic predictions. [14]
Conversely, theoretical work on connectome-constrained recurrent networks shows that the
same connectivity can permit substantially different dynamics; recordings from a subset of
neurons can reduce that ambiguity. [18] [19]

Eon: embodiment, not magic
Eon’s March technical description identifies steering readouts such as DNa01/DNa02, a forward-
velocity interface associated with oDN1, antennal-grooming outputs, and proboscis motor
activity; selected outputs influence lower-level body controllers rather than directly
reconstructing the full motor hierarchy. Technical description
That account also states that walking relies on existing imitation-trained controllers, many
brain–body mappings were chosen by hand, and the implemented visual activity did not yet
substantially influence demonstrated behavioral outputs. These qualifications substantially
narrow the interpretation of the promotional “uploaded fly” language. Technical description
Useful achievement: integration of measured neural architecture into a closed loop.
Not established: recovery of an individual fly, general independent learning, full brain-to-
muscle emulation, or subjective experience.

Failures worth preserving
Three failures directly inform Neural Art Lab:
  1. A learning rule cannot rescue nondiscriminative sensory representations. Fly Brain Bench
     reports that its tested olfactory model activated highly overlapping Kenyon-cell
     populations across different odors, preventing a defensible conditioning interpretation.
     Repository diagnostics
  2. Changed weights are not evidence of improved behavior. DOOMFLY explicitly preserves
     plasticity candidates that failed conditioning and survival tests. [24] [2]
  3. A biological graph can tie or lose to controls. NeuroTerrarium reports no advantage in its
     default real-microcircuit pulse assay, and its synthetic recurrent fixture did not outperform
     the relevant alternatives. Repository evidence
These are useful experimental outcomes, not embarrassments to hide.

Other species
Complete connectomic resources also exist for much smaller nervous systems, including
worms, but the BANC paper’s comparison does not by itself establish the fidelity of any
particular worm-to-robot demonstration. [12]
This review did not establish a sufficiently audited cross-species robot-control inventory to
attach confident neuron counts, learning claims, and null-model results to those
demonstrations. They remain a gap rather than evidence that should be filled with familiar
internet anecdotes.

What can transfer?
The strongest candidates are useful transformations, not complete instincts:
     Left–right differentiation.
     Stimulus-triggered state transitions.
     Competition between channels.
     Temporal filtering.
     Thresholding and amplification.
     Context-dependent gating.
     Recurrent state that can help distinguish histories.
Some such transformations already support feeding, grooming, pursuit, escape-related, and
visual-motion models, although each result is specific to its model and assay. Shiu paper [14] [1]
A flight-escape pathway might become an effective material-release trigger. It does not thereby
acquire an evolved understanding of fractal extrusion.

When the body is mathematics
A body need not be a mesh. It can be the part of the environment over which the controller has
bounded, persistent influence.
The following proposed definition is useful:
        An operational body is a controllable process with state, constraints, action costs, and
        feedback—not necessarily a contiguous object.
That permits:
    A boundary separating two materials.
    A set of field singularities.
    A dynamically maintained graph.
    A scheduling process that allocates simulation time.
    A distributed pigment gradient.
    A collection of persistent edits.
    A local rule that changes interactions between other objects.

A proposed causality test
Record one state, then branch the simulation under:
 1. Actual neural output.
 2. Zero output.
 3. Time-shuffled output.
 4. Output from another organism.
 5. A matched scripted controller.
 6. A degree/sign/weight-matched rewired network.
 7. A conventional recurrent network with a comparable trainable budget.
Measure the divergence of task-relevant outcomes, not just image differences.
An image can change dramatically because one threshold crossed. That demonstrates
sensitivity, not necessarily competent control.


Artificial senses
A sensor can report a mathematical property that has no natural sensory organ. The difficult
part is choosing an encoding that the neural model can distinguish and an adaptation
mechanism that can exploit it.
The proposal should always name both halves:
          “Computed curvature is encoded as input to population X.”
Not:
          “Population X naturally perceives curvature.”

Sparkles becoming branching
A technically coherent proposed pathway is:
  1. The organism initially receives high reward for structured sparkle.
  2. Repeated sparkle production increases feature-specific satiation.
  3. Branching structures become useful because they gather material or create new
     controllable surfaces.
  4. Successful branching reduces prediction error and improves resource access.
  5. A learned value model assigns greater future value to actions leading to branching.
  6. That preference persists when short-term satiation is removed.
  7. It transfers to unfamiliar branching examples.
This would support an experience-dependent functional preference. It would not establish
pleasure, aesthetic experience, or a subjective desire for branches.

What would count as adaptation?
A proposed minimum test battery:
     Acquisition: behavior changes with relevant experience.
     Retention: the change survives a pause or restart.
     Specificity: unrelated stimuli do not change identically.
     Reversal: changed contingencies can change the policy.
     Transfer: learning generalizes beyond exact training scenes.
     Reset: restoring the old learned state restores earlier behavior.
     Controls: frozen, shuffled-reward, and matched random-policy conditions differ
     appropriately.
Two identical deterministic copies will not diverge under identical inputs and state updates.
Different environments, stochastic histories, or learning perturbations can produce different
histories; persistent divergence must then be measured rather than inferred from different-
looking pictures.

Mushroom-body caution
A mushroom-body-inspired plasticity rule is attractive, but the presence of Kenyon cells,
MBONs, and dopamine annotations is not sufficient to guarantee a functioning conditioning
model. Both DOOMFLY and Fly Brain Bench provide examples where missing or unsuitable
dynamics obstruct the intended learning interpretation. Diagnostics [2] [24]
For Neural Art Lab, readout learning is the lower-risk first experiment. A biologically
motivated mushroom-body module should be introduced as a separate research track with its
own sensory-sparsity and conditioning benchmarks.

Emergent specialization
To test “one becomes a pigment manipulator and another a structural sculptor,” do not assign
those roles in the action set.

heterogeneous resources, and permit persistent experience. Measure whether specialization
appears across multiple runs and whether it disappears when the relevant ecological
asymmetry is removed.
A colony may create more varied art simply because it has more actuators and more random
sources. Compare against a single controller with the same total action budget before
attributing the difference to collective intelligence.

Two different null-model questions
Causal dependence: Does this trained organism’s behavior depend on its current biological
network?
Test acute silencing, disconnection, scrambling, or replay substitution while holding the
decoder fixed.
Architectural usefulness: Is the biological network a better substrate than alternatives?
Retrain matched decoders separately on biological, shuffled, random, and conventional
recurrent substrates using equal training and parameter budgets.
These are not interchangeable. A decoder failing after its inputs are scrambled proves
dependence on its learned feature distribution; it does not by itself prove that biological
topology is superior.

Important mathematical distinctions
A proposed simulation should not casually treat all attractive patterns as equivalent “physics.”
    A conserved composition field and a nonconserved growth field need different accounting.
    An aperiodic tiling is not automatically a quasicrystal.
    A fractal renderer is not automatically an interactive dynamical environment.
    A fluid-inspired shader is not automatically a validated fluid solver.
    A topology-changing mesh is mathematically legitimate without representing physically
    possible matter.

Memory arithmetic
These are derived storage estimates, not measured application benchmarks:
    A sparse edge using a 32-bit target index and a 32-bit weight costs approximately 8 bytes
    before offsets, delays, and overhead.
    About 25.6 million such edges require approximately 205 MB for those two arrays alone.
    Per-edge plasticity state, eligibility traces, duplicated CPU/GPU buffers, and optimizer state
    can multiply that requirement.
    A dense 166,700-by-166,700 matrix would be an entirely different and generally
    inappropriate representation.
Actual working memory must be profiled on the chosen backend.

High-resolution export
Yes: the simulation need not run at final image resolution.
Proposed export architecture:
 1. Simulate material dynamics on a lower-resolution grid.
 2. Store vector contours, particle seeds, geometry, and material state.
 3. Save deterministic checkpoints and action logs.
 4. Rerender offline at the desired resolution.
 5. Advance simulation by fixed steps per exported frame.
 6. Encode image sequences locally into GIF or MP4 using an appropriate local encoder.
Do not upscale a low-resolution state and claim newly resolved physical detail. High-resolution
shading, contour reconstruction, antialiasing, and geometric rendering can improve
appearance without changing the underlying simulation.

Proposed world layers
  1. Pigment transport: mobile colored substances.
  2. Contour field: persistent strata reconstructed from deposited material or level sets.
  3. Crystal growth: material-limited seeding and branching.
  4. Wave field: interference and excitable events.
  5. Flow: transports pigment and redistributes growth resources.
  6. Structure: barriers, membranes, and attachment points.
  7. History: deposits, damage, and dormant seeds remain.

Bounded intervention vocabulary
Start with a small action space:
    Move.
    Deposit pigment.
    Consume material.
    Add a local force.
    Seed growth.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 31 / 48>
    Inhibit growth.
    Attach a filament.
    Trigger a wave.
    Repair a boundary.
    Wait.
The organism should choose site, strength, and duration, with explicit costs and cooldowns.

Initial tastes without permanent obedience
Separate:
    Initial aesthetic prior: your preferred visual features.
    Viability objective: persistent resource constraints.
    Learned value: predicted future consequences.
    Familiarity: reduces repetitive reward.
    Exploration: bounded investigation of uncertain effects.
Store these separately so that a claimed taste change can be traced to actual experience rather
than an invisible update to a single “personality” number.

What makes combinations unplanned?
A crystal can redirect flow; redirected flow changes pigment deposition; pigment inhibits one
growth process while feeding another; new contours alter the sensory field; the agent then
chooses a different intervention.
No single action needs an authored final image. The surprise comes from interaction among
specified processes.

Failure modes
    One coupling destabilizes every field.
    The agent repeatedly triggers the most visually salient effect.
    The garden becomes permanently saturated.
    Long-delayed consequences defeat learning.
    Rendering features leak privileged world state into the policy.
    A planner does all meaningful site selection while neural output only changes intensity.

Validation
Compare:
    Coupled versus uncoupled materials.
    Persistent versus periodically reset history.

     Neural versus scripted intervention selection.
     Learned versus frozen preference systems.
     Biological versus matched alternative neural substrates.
     One agent versus two agents with equal total actuation.
Short-term: pigment + contour + one crystal process.
Long-term: ecological resource cycles, external memory, and bounded rule editing.
