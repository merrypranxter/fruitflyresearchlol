1. Executive Scientific Reality Check: The Anatomy of
a Simulated Fly Brain
As of late 2026, the term "simulated fly brain" encompasses several distinct tiers of
neurobiological data, mathematical approximation, and computational abstraction. It is critical to
precisely delineate these tiers, as conflating a static wiring diagram with an independently
learning artificial agent leads to profound engineering and epistemological errors. The
foundational anatomical basis of these simulations rests on dense electron-microscopy
reconstructions of Drosophila melanogaster.
The primary structural datasets currently driving the field include the FlyWire (FAFB)
connectome, finalized and published in Nature in October 2024, which mapped the complete
adult female brain, encompassing approximately 139,255 neurons and tens of millions of
synapses. However, FlyWire lacks the ventral nerve cord (the fly analog to a spinal cord),
leaving a gap in direct motor actuation. This limitation was resolved by the MaleCNS dataset,
released by HHMI Janelia, Cambridge, and Google Research (v1.0 in June 2026, published in
Cell in September 2026), which contains over 166,000 neurons and roughly 125 million
synapses, crucially encompassing both the complete brain and the ventral nerve cord. This
allows for seamless brain-to-leg and brain-to-wing motor pathway tracing without forcing
researchers to guess how descending neurons (DNs) innervate motor centers. Concurrently, the
BANC (Brain and Nerve Cord) project provided a whole-CNS connectome for the adult female.
Platforms like FlyWire Codex, neuPrint, and neuprint-python provide programmatic access to
these massive graphs, allowing researchers to query specific cell types, fetch adjacency
matrices, and trace multi-hop pathways.
However, a connectome is fundamentally a static wiring diagram—a list of nodes and weighted
directed edges. To transition from a structural graph to a functional organism, significant
engineering assumptions are required. Eckstein et al. (2024) bridged a major gap by utilizing
machine learning to classify neurotransmitter identities from electron microscopy images,
predicting whether a synapse is excitatory (e.g., cholinergic) or inhibitory (e.g., GABAergic or
glutamatergic). Building upon this, Shiu et al. (2024) pioneered a whole-brain leaky
integrate-and-fire (LIF) model constrained by the FlyWire connectome. In this framework,
neurons are simulated as mathematical capacitors that accumulate voltage from incoming
spikes and discharge when a threshold is crossed. Shiu demonstrated that activating specific
gustatory receptor neurons (GRNs) in the simulated graph accurately predicted the activation of
motor neuron MN9, which governs proboscis extension in the biological fly.
The distinction between differing simulation paradigms is stark and dictates what is genuinely
possible. A static wiring diagram merely shows potential routing. A mathematically simulated
spiking network passes electrical signals based on fixed rules but lacks long-term learning. A
connectome-informed behavioral controller extracts a functional subcircuit (like the E-PG ring
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 2 / 26>
attractor for heading) and uses it as a topological prior for a trained neural network. A complete
embodied sensorimotor loop, such as Eon Systems' virtual fruit fly or NeuroMechFly, links these
simulated spikes to a biomechanical physics engine (like MuJoCo), where simulated
environments drive sensory neurons, which propagate spikes to motor neurons, actuating
physical joints that subsequently alter the sensory input. Advanced models integrate synaptic
plasticity, implementing dopamine-gated weight updates in the mushroom body (MB) to allow
continuous learning.
Current simulations still lack massive amounts of biological reality. They generally omit gap
junctions (electrical synapses), complex multi-compartment dendritic computation, diverse
ion-channel dynamics, volumetric neuromodulation (hormonal baths), glial cell interactions, and
intrinsic spontaneous firing mechanics. An LIF simulation does not spontaneously generate
directed behavior without external sensory stimulation; it requires an engineered environment to
close the loop. Speculative claims regarding whole-brain emulation or computational
consciousness remain unsupported, whereas functional sensorimotor
transformations—mapping defined inputs to ecologically valid outputs—are robustly
demonstrated.

Analysis of Failures and Assumptions: Many high-profile demonstrations (e.g., chess
applications) utilize the connectome merely as a static, randomized reservoir. If a randomly
shuffled control network performs equally well, the biological topology is not contributing to the
intelligence; the system is merely exploiting high-dimensional random projections. The most
robust and scientifically legitimate demonstrations—such as Shiu et al.'s feeding circuit and the
c3s-reflex-circuits (giant fiber escape)—prove that specific, evolutionarily conserved subgraphs
(like LC4 and LPLC2 inputs converging on the DNp01 giant fiber) reliably execute complex
logical computations (e.g., differentiating looming velocity from absolute size). For these circuits,
the specific biological wiring is irreplaceable.

3. Anatomical Freedom: The Body Problem and the
Neural Octopus
The central inquiry is whether a biological nervous system can meaningfully control an actuator
system with completely divergent anatomy. Biological brains exhibit immense morphological
abstraction. Central pattern generators (CPGs) in the ventral nerve cord abstract high-level
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 5 / 26>
descending signals from the brain into complex, multi-joint rhythmic actuations.
A simulated fly brain outputs behavior via a narrow informational bottleneck: roughly 1,300
Descending Neurons (DNs) traversing the neck connective. If we discard the fly's native
biomechanics, these DNs become abstract, low-dimensional control channels. Known functional
mappings include:
   ●​ DNg100 / DNp09: Command forward walking.
   ●​ DNa02 / DNg13: Govern yaw and steering by differentially acting on left/right motor
       circuits.
   ●​ DNp01 (Giant Fiber) / DNp04: Trigger rapid, ballistic escape mechanisms.
Experiment A: The Neural Octopus Architecture To control an eight-armed, soft-bodied
octopus, the system cannot map a fly's alternating-tripod gait directly to tentacles. It
necessitates a hierarchical, reinforcement-trained intermediary controller.
   1.​ Sensory Remapping: The octopus's optical input is encoded onto the fly's retinotopic
       optic lobes. Eight continuous tactile arrays from the tentacles are dimensionally reduced
       and mapped to the fly's mechanosensory bristles (e.g., Johnston's organ or leg
       proprioceptors).
   2.​ Action Decoding: The fly brain processes these inputs and issues commands via DNs. A
       looming visual threat triggers DNp01. A lateral target triggers DNa02 (steering).
   3.​ Engineered Intermediary Controllers: These low-dimensional DN spikes are fed into an
       artificial Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) acting as a synthetic CPG. This decoder
       interprets DNg100 (forward speed) and expands it into the thousands of inverse-kinematic
       motor commands required to undulate eight soft tentacles.
   4.​ Local Subcontrollers (Morphological Computation): Octopuses possess highly
       distributed nervous systems. The artificial body must contain local reflex loops. If the fly
       brain issues a "steer left" command via DNa02, the left arms autonomously execute
       grasping physics, while the right arms push, handled entirely by the local physics
       simulation.
   5.​ Learning: True biological synaptic plasticity (e.g., Mushroom Body DAN-gating) allows
       the fly to associate a specific visual pattern with a target. The artificial decoder utilizes
       standard neuroevolution to learn how to translate DN activity into efficient tentacle
       movement over thousands of generations.
The resulting organism possesses the intent, valence, and reflex latencies of an insect, but the
physical execution of a cephalopod.

4. Abstract Embodiment: When the Body is a
Mathematical Universe
If motor outputs need not control anatomical muscles, they can serve as continuous forcing
functions within abstract dynamical systems. The nervous system transitions from a
biomechanical driver to a parameter-space navigator.
Examples of Abstract Bodies:
   ●​ Mathematical Bodies: Spiking outputs directly manipulate variables in non-linear partial
       differential equations. For instance, right-hemisphere DNg13 activity increases the
       diffusion coefficient in a Reaction-Diffusion system, while left-hemisphere DNa02 alters
       the feed rate, generating shifting Turing patterns as a form of "movement."
   ●​ Particle Bodies: Utilizing the Material Point Method (MPM), an organism can exist as a
       fluid swarm of 100,000 particles. The fly's motor outputs exert directional force fields.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 6 / 26>
       Forward walking (DNg100) causes the particles to flow as a cohesive liquid. An escape
       reflex (DNp01) triggers a phase-change, exploding the body into a diffuse gas.
  ●​ Temporal Bodies: Neural activity modulates the simulation's chronological tick rate or
       alters the decay rate of historical rendering trails, allowing the organism to navigate
       temporal accumulation rather than spatial geometry.
To distinguish a mathematically legitimate neural-to-world interface from a superficial visualizer,
the system must be strictly closed-loop: the mathematical output must deterministically generate
the subsequent sensory input. A genuine interface requires the mathematical environment to
possess chaotic or complex non-linear dynamics (e.g., Navier-Stokes flow), ensuring the brain's
actions produce unpredictable environmental states that the brain must actively continuously
rectify.

5. Engineering the Impossible Alien Organism
(Experiment B)
The user proposed an organism that eats pixels, digests colors, excretes glitter, and explodes
into geometry. This is fundamentally engineering-feasible when modeled as a multi-modal
finite-state machine guided by the connectome's latent behaviors.
The Glitter-Eating Alien Architecture:
   1.​ Eating Pixels (Sensory to Internal State): The organism roams a 2D canvas, rendered
        via WebGPU. The fly's visual system processes the pixels. When the fly navigates toward
        a dense pixel cluster, its gustatory receptor neurons (GRNs) are artificially stimulated.
   2.​ Digesting Colors (LIF Accumulation): GRN spikes propagate to the feeding circuit (e.g.,
        Rattle, Zorro neurons). We engineer a synthetic "metabolic stomach" variable. As feeding
        motor neuron MN9 spikes, pixels are deleted from the canvas, and their RGB data is

   3.​ Excreting Glitter (Motor Output to MPM Physics): When the stomach variable crosses
        a threshold, or when grooming behavior is triggered (aDN1 activity), the organism expels

        structure, LC4 and LPLC2 neurons trigger the Giant Fiber (DNp01). Instead of a physical
        jump, this DNp01 spike triggers a mathematical singularity—spawning a branching fractal
        and instantly displacing the organism's coordinate location.

6. Synthetic Sensory Modalities and Perception
Biological brains map physical phenomena (photons, pressure, airborne molecules) to spike
trains. An artificial encoder can theoretically map arbitrary mathematical geometries to those
exact same spike trains.
Mapping the Impossible:
   ●​ Mathematical Curvature: The local Gaussian curvature of a 3D generative dataset can
       be mapped to the fly's mechanosensory bristle neurons. A highly wrinkled topology
       physically "feels" rough to the connectome.
   ●​ Entropy and Novelty: A continuous measure of environmental compressibility (Shannon
       entropy) can be fed into the dopaminergic system (PPL1 or PAM neurons), simulating

        intrinsic curiosity or pain based on data novelty relative to previous states.
    ●​ Organism Proximity: Mapping the Euclidean distance of other synthetic creatures
        directly to olfactory projection neurons (PNs), translating spatial metrics into a gradient of
        scent.
A brain could theoretically interpret arbitrary mappings if it possesses sufficient plasticity.
However, the static fly connectome expects specific spatial topologies. Feeding non-spatial data
(like raw audio frequencies) directly into the retinotopic optic lobe will produce predictable but
biologically nonsensical neural cascades. To engineer true perception of novel stimuli, one must
interface with the Mushroom Body (MB). Here, Kenyon Cells (KCs) naturally perform
high-dimensional, randomized feature expansion, making the architecture highly suited for
learning arbitrary, non-biological associations.

7. Desire, Motivation, and Evolving Aesthetic
Appetites
To prevent the organism from being permanently forced to reproduce human-authored aesthetic
preferences (e.g., violently vivid neon colors, Fordite strata, structured sparkles), the system
must leverage the insect's intrinsic learning and motivation centers to evolve its own tastes.
Biological Grounding: In Drosophila, valence (good/bad associations) and memory are
encoded in the Mushroom Body. Sensory inputs activate sparse ensembles of Kenyon Cells.
These KCs synapse onto Mushroom Body Output Neurons (MBONs), which dictate approach or
avoidance behaviors. The synaptic weights between KCs and MBONs are dynamically modified
by Dopaminergic Neurons (DANs) representing reward or punishment. Furthermore, models of
Active Inference demonstrate that biological networks strive to minimize variational free energy
(predictive error).
Engineering Evolving Aesthetics:
   1.​ Initial Artificial Drives (The Baseline): We engineer a software-based "aesthetic sensor"
       that evaluates the canvas for the user's initial preferences (e.g., fractal dimension,
       reaction-diffusion contrast). High scores artificially stimulate PAM DANs (reward); low
       scores stimulate PPL1 DANs (punishment).
   2.​ Associative Learning: As the organism interacts with the environment, the KC-to-MBON
       weights undergo Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP). The organism learns which
       specific motor sequences (e.g., steering into dense phase-separation zones) reliably
       produce the "rewarding" neon colors.
   3.​ Active Inference and Boredom: By dynamically shifting the reward signal from absolute
       aesthetic value to predictive error (rewarding the organism when the environment
       behaves unexpectedly), the organism develops intrinsic motivation. It will initially seek
       sparkles, master how to create them, experience a drop in predictive error ("boredom"),
       and subsequently alter its behavior to explore new topological geometries.
   4.​ Divergent Personalities: Two identical connectomes placed in slightly different
       mathematical environments will develop completely different KC-MBON weight
       distributions through experience, resulting in divergent, persistent aesthetic "tastes."

9. Morphological Freedom: Altering the Biological
Connectome
Maintaining strict biological fidelity restricts the organism to insectoid reflexes. Significant
engineering freedom is achieved by altering the graph structure itself.
  ●​ Unmodified Connectome (Demonstrated): Running the MaleCNS as-is, using only
      environmental interaction for feedback, which ensures maximum biological validity.
  ●​ Targeted Silencing and Rewiring (Research Frontier): We can selectively ablate the
      LPLC2 neurons to make the organism "blind" to certain geometric expansions, or
      artificially multiply the synaptic weights of the steering neurons (DNa02) to induce
      hyper-sensitivity to directional shifts.
  ●​ Hybridization (Speculative but Feasible): Splicing the output of the biological FlyWire
      central complex directly into a PyTorch-based Deep Reinforcement Learning module. The
      biological tissue provides an incredibly efficient heading-direction compass (the E-PG ring
      attractor), while the artificial neural network learns to use this biological primitive to
      navigate high-dimensional manifolds.
  ●​ Validation: To prove the biological wiring actively contributes to the emergent art, every
      experiment must be evaluated against a control model where the synaptic matrix is
      degree-preserved but randomly shuffled. If the shuffled brain creates identical art, the
      biological topology is functioning merely as a complex random number generator.

12. Computational Feasibility (Neural Art Lab Context)
The current neural_art_lab successfully utilizes a 6,203-neuron subcircuit running LIF in
standard JavaScript. However, to scale to the full MaleCNS (166,000 neurons, 125M synapses)
alongside complex mathematical physics (MPM, fluids), the architecture must fundamentally
evolve.
   ●​ Mobile Browsers (WebGPU): A modern mobile device with WebGPU can comfortably
      run tens of thousands of LIF neurons via compute shaders. The primary bottleneck is
      memory bandwidth—transferring 125M synaptic weights to the GPU VRAM per frame is
      impossible. The matrix must be persistently stored in a sparse CSR (Compressed Sparse
      Row) format in VRAM.
   ●​ Physics/Neural Decoupling: The system must utilize disparate timescales. The LIF
      neural simulation requires 0.1ms to 1ms internal timesteps to maintain mathematical
      stability for spike integration. The visual physics (MPM, Reaction-Diffusion) can run at a
      standard 16ms (60 FPS) rendering loop.
   ●​ Taichi.js and MPM: For rendering particles, crystalline growth, and soft bodies directly in
      the browser, Taichi.js allows Python-like syntax to be compiled into high-performance
      WebAssembly or WebGPU compute shaders. This is ideal for the Neural Art Lab,
      enabling millions of particles to interact with the neural network locally without server
      costs.
   ●​ Desktop/Native Environments: Full whole-brain LIF coupled with multi-million particle
      MPM currently strains mobile thermals but is easily handled by desktop Apple Silicon (via
      Metal) or discrete RTX GPUs (via CUDA or WebGPU backends like Nexus).

13. The Strangest Legitimate Possibilities
Moving into the theoretical domains of Artificial Life (ALife) and extended cognition reveals
radical possibilities for connectome deployment.
  ●​ Self-Modifying Morphology: If the connectome controls a modular robotic swarm, the
      organism can physically reassemble its anatomy based on failure states. A "walking"
      circuit might fail, causing the organism to rearrange its particles into a continuous tread,
      utilizing the same neural rhythmic pulses to roll rather than walk.
  ●​ Niche Construction and Extended Cognition: The organism secretes mathematical
      boundaries (e.g., drawing lines in a physics engine). It builds a structure that funnels
      interesting visual stimuli toward its sensors. The organism's cognition is now extended
      into the environment; it relies on the physical structure to filter its sensory input, reducing
      computational load.
  ●​ Programmable Matter (Neural Cellular Automata): The brain acts as a central hub, but
      every pixel of the "body" is an independent cellular automaton. The brain sends a single

     command spike, and the matter independently ripples, crystalizes, or computes a
     distributed algorithm to execute the action.
  ●​ Metabolic Simulation: The organism requires a synthetic metabolism. Running the LIF
     simulation depletes a global "energy" variable. To survive, the organism must navigate a
     nutrient gradient (color fields) to recharge its computational capacity. Failure results in
     localized synaptic dropout (simulated cell death), fundamentally altering its artistic output

15. Assumption Destruction: Expanding the Ontology
of the Organism
To discover fundamentally new design spaces, we must systematically destroy the
anthropomorphic and biological assumptions embedded in classical neuroscience:
  ●​ Assumption: A brain must have one body.
        ○​ Destruction: The connectome is a mathematical matrix. We can duplicate the motor
            output vector and pipe it into a swarm of 1,000 particles simultaneously. The
            organism is no longer an entity; it is a distributed field of action.
  ●​ Assumption: Bodies must have permanent anatomy.
        ○​ Destruction: Allow the organism to dynamically allocate limbs based on sensory
            input. If the visual field requires more manipulation, the motor signals instantiate
            new inverse-kinematic chains on the fly. The body becomes a temporary expression
            of computational need.
  ●​ Assumption: Limbs must exert physical forces.
        ○​ Destruction: Motor outputs modulate the rules of the simulation, not physical objects
            within it. A spike from DNg13 doesn't turn a body; it rotates the gravity vector of the
            entire universe. The organism remains perfectly stationary while the mathematical
            world revolves around it.
  ●​ Assumption: Memory must exist exclusively inside biological synapses.
        ○​ Destruction: The organism can use the environment as an external hard drive
            (Extended Cognition). It carves topographic trails into the digital canvas. When it
            crosses the trail later, the curvature stimulates its mechanoreceptors. It
            "remembers" by reading its own scars on the world, reducing the need for internal
            plasticity.
  ●​ Assumption: An agent must have a recognizable head or central core.
        ○​ Destruction: Consider a topological organism that is an infinite 1D string stretching
            across a 3D environment. The "brain" exists nowhere physically; the string simply
            contracts, knots, and vibrates based on the LIF matrix evaluation occurring
            off-screen.

17. Development Roadmap for Neural Art Lab
The current Neural Art Lab (https://github.com/merrypranxter/neural_art_lab) successfully
utilizes a 6,203-neuron subcircuit running LIF in standard JavaScript. To scale this while
preserving stability, the following staged roadmap is recommended:
Phase 1: WebGPU Translation (Near-Term, Low Risk)
   ●​ Action: Port the existing 6,203-neuron LIF simulation from CPU JavaScript to a WebGPU
       compute shader. Keep the exact same sensory inputs (looming left/right) and outputs.
   ●​ Why: This eliminates the CPU bottleneck, allowing the simulation to scale to 166,000
       neurons without freezing the UI thread.
   ●​ Hardware: Compatible with current iPhone browsers and desktop.
Phase 2: Full Connectome Integration (Medium-Term)
   ●​ Action: Replace the 6,203-neuron subset with the neuPrint MaleCNS v1.0 dataset.
   ●​ Engineered Component: Implement sparse matrix multiplication (CSR format) in WebGPU
       to handle the 125M synapses efficiently.
   ●​ Result: The organism gains access to the full sensory suite (vision, olfactory, taste) and
       the complete Mushroom Body learning centers for true biological plasticity.
Phase 3: Native MPM Physics (Long-Term)
   ●​ Action: Integrate Taichi.js or a custom WebGPU MPM (Material Point Method) pipeline.
   ●​ Why: To replace the current 2D canvas effects with genuine, computationally accurate
       fluid, particle, and soft-body dynamics, enabling the "Glitter-Eating Alien" and "Octopus"
       architectures natively in the browser without relying on superficial particle libraries.
