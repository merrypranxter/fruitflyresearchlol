# P03 — Eight-Arm Stirrer

**Research source:** R04 pp. 24–28; [full text](../raw-text/R04-continuation-after-72.txt).
**Status:** design proposal.

**Question:** Does fly-derived neural modulation improve controlled pigment manipulation using an eight-arm, mechanically local body?

## Minimal body
Fixed/slow central hub, eight planar elastic multi-node arms, bend/stretch constraints, damping, contact, optional adhesion. Local arms own passive elastic return, contact reflex and optional oscillator. Their actions must be separately logged as engineered.

## Neural interface
Verify present neural populations. Try a small synergy signal for extension, contraction, angular bias, stirring strength and adhesion. Do not assume eight independent biological motor commands exist. Independent arm addressing, if added, is an explicit artificial sequential address-and-hold system.

## First tasks
One task at a time: move pigment into a region, mix two reservoirs, keep two reservoirs separated, or clear a particle path. Freeze connectome; optionally train tiny central readout. No full dexterity/3D muscle simulation needed at first.

## Controls
Passive mechanics; local reflexes only; oscillator only; neural modulation; amplitude/bandwidth-matched random modulation; matched rewired circuit; fixed/constant neural substitute.

## Measures and acceptance
Task-relevant material displacement or mixing, cost, arm diversity, contact stability, robustness to changed material layout, arm removal/identity swaps, local reflex ablations. **Pass only when** neural modulation improves function beyond the non-neural local system and generalizes.

## Failure risks
All arms synchronized, local oscillator doing everything, neural signal merely amplifying visual noise, one arm dominating geometry, solver instability and local stabilizer overwhelming neural effects.
